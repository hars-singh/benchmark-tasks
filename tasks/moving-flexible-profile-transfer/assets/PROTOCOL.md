# Flexible insulating profile transfer

The three source saddles support a long narrow polyamide channel while the carriage translates. The robot grasps the two end spans, lifts and moves the channel sideways to the receiving rack. Measuring peak bend during the whole trial catches a transfer that briefly sags or buckles before it is placed. The center receiving saddle starts 0.45 mm above the end saddles and compresses against a spring as the channel settles. This lets the three saddles share the load despite small placement and alignment differences. Manufacturer guidance for polyamide insulation profiles describes avoiding bending and twisting during support and handling. The bench geometry and nominal flexure settings are specific to this task. [Technoform material and handling guidance](https://www.technoform.com/sites/default/files/2025-05/quality_documents_en_03.25.pdf#page=19)

The OpenSCAD file gives the complete reset geometry in millimetres. The JSON also specifies materials, mass, the native rail and profile joints, and the exact source digest. A physical fixture can use a comparable polyamide channel and padded saddles. Put visible straight witness marks on each of the three spans. Use synchronized overhead and side views to measure elevation, maximum bend, and wrist clearance. Small force sensors under the three receiving pads can confirm that the weight is distributed after release. The scored 0.026 N per pad is within the range of a scale with a 1 g display increment when each pad can be measured separately.

The simulation approximates bending with two axis-limited springs. A physical strip may twist or develop a distributed bend. Record those effects in a physical trial and inspect any permanent deformation before reusing the strip.
