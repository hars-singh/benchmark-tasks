# Guided pinion removal

A light service carriage brings an unpowered motor through the work zone. The left wrist seats the fork around the shaft behind the pinion. The right wrist depresses a separate wedge. The wedge’s follower translates the crosshead and draws the gear forward with the fork. The tray receives the gear once it clears the shaft. Commercial compact pinion pullers similarly use a solid seat behind the pinion and apply axial force to separate it from the shaft. The bench fixture uses a vertically operated wedge and a separately seated fork. [Tamiya reference puller](https://www.tamiya.com/english/products/15422/index.html), [REV pinion service guide](https://docs.revrobotics.com/duo-build/motion/motors/hd-hex-motor/pinion-removal-guide)

Build the fixture from the supplied JSON and OpenSCAD geometry. The carriage is aluminum. The fork, crosshead, slide, wedge and follower use steel bearing surfaces. Fit a replaceable steel 18 mm gear and 6 mm shaft with a nominal 10 N breakout reaction for bench testing. Screen each shaft and gear batch with an inline pull scale and replace tested samples that have loosened. The two arms only contact the fork press roller and wedge pad. The unpowered motor shell, tray and guides travel with the carriage.

Place a ruler alongside the rail and record both hands, the fork, the exposed shaft and the teal tray. The gear must clear the shaft, stay inside the tray and move with the carriage after both hands withdraw. The simulation grades native contact reactions and a surrogate interference bond. A physical trial can use the recorded fork seating and extraction sequence, the visible shaft clearance and the recovered gear as its outcome checks. The workpiece dimensions and 10 N rating define this bench test.
