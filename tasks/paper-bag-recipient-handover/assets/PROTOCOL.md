# Hand over a loaded paper shopping bag

Use an unprinted Biopack TB0180822 paper bag or the same 180 × 80 × 220 mm gusseted bag with two twisted paper handles. Use a lightly loaded bag with two closed cartons at 100 g and 85 g. Use the two setup marks on the regular bench and the overhead recording to check distances. Align the bag's long axis across the bench. At reset both cartons are upright and the empty gap between the handle crowns is approximately 18 mm, without clips.

Mark the handover location at x=388 mm, y=-112 mm in station coordinates. Measure the 283 mm palm path from y=260 mm to y=-23 mm at x=366 mm. The test recipient keeps their fingers curled upward and threaded through both loops as the arm withdraws horizontally. The same recipient uses the same marked path on each trial. Use a side view to check that the bag clears the table by at least 50 mm throughout the final hold.

Once the recipient has pulled back 160 mm, examine the next continuous second on the recording. Both loops must visibly rest on the recipient's fingers, both cartons must remain inside and neither robot wrist may continue to support the bag. The partial score compares both handles and the bag location with the setup marks. Use a ruler at the mark to check the separation of the handle crowns and the table clearance.

The bag shell and each loop use native rigid geometry, and the two loops have individual compliant attachment pivots. The two cartons are free bodies within the shell. The hand's dimensions approximate an adult palm and include separate collision segments for four curled fingers and the thumb. The palm motion uses a driven prismatic joint and never sets a bag or handle pose.
