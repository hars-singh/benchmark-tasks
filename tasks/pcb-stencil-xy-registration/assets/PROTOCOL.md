# Register a PCB stencil before printing

Use the standard SMTmax YSJ-250 printer with a framed stainless stencil and regular board stops. Align the frame hinge at the back of the station. Orient the X screw along the table depth and the Y screw across it. The station coordinates of the nominal coupon centre are x=443 mm and y=-18 mm. Use the same adjustments on each trial.

Use a 90 × 112 × 1.6 mm FR-4 coupon. Place square 5.5 mm copper lands at x=−14 and +14 mm and y=−37.5, −12.5, +12.5 and +37.5 mm relative to the coupon centre. The 0.25 mm stencil has square 4.2 mm apertures at those same centres. Set the in-plane reset offset to −0.95 mm in X and +0.70 mm in Y using a graticule or digital microscope. Use a 1.7 mm dry inspection clearance from pad top to foil. The stencil starts seated.

With a vertical close view, compare each aperture centre with its matching land centre. The allowed displacement is 0.38 mm in the plane, verified from the land edges or the symmetry of exposed copper. Establish scale with the 25 mm separation between neighbouring pad rows. Measure the frame opening with a side view and angle card. A full score needs at least 21 degrees opening, repeat closure, one second of alignment, and both grippers clear of the rail by 80 mm.

The native carriages move independently on the two adjustment axes. An angular-to-linear coupling models each screw with a nominal 1.8 mm travel per revolution. The physical printer's actual thread pitch affects the number of turns required. The task compares resulting registration. Dry inspection uses a coupon without any paste or squeegee.
