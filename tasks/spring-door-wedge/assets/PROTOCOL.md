# Hold a spring-hinged door open with a rubber wedge

Use a light, non-rated indoor passage or workshop door mounted on a Deltana DSH35 series single-action spring hinge. Set spring tension so the empty door closes on its own when released from 65 degrees. Use a Shepherd Hardware 9163 rubber wedge, nominally 4.75 by 1.25 by 1.50 inches. The underside of the door should clear the hard, flat floor by 17 mm in the tabletop scene. Put the wedge within reach with its toe and heel unobstructed.

Record the door from above with angle marks for 50, 56, 61 and 66 degrees from closed. Open beyond 66 degrees against the spring. Place the loose wedge so that the door settles against the ramp as it moves toward closed. Check a side view of the toe and bottom rail. Let the wedge settle under the spring load. After both hands withdraw, verify that it drifts no more than 2 mm for two seconds and record the final angle. Reset the wedge to its starting mark and allow the door to close.

The simulator uses a scaled native rigid-body panel, a spring-loaded hinge drive and a loose colliding rubber wedge. The support comes from contact. Replace the spring drive with the stock hinge in a physical trial, and use a removable wedge only with an ordinary non-rated workshop door.
