# Propeller trim-weight and balance fixture

The fixture first lets the propeller swing freely so the heavier blade drops. After the fixture returns to its application position, the robot uses a gold tamp pad to press a measured trim strip onto the lighter blade. A steel anvil supports the blade during pressing. The pad retracts and the foil stays physically supported at the application point. The fixture then turns the rotor through a bounded sweep, releases its drive, and records the corrected unpowered drift. Applying small tape pieces to a lighter nylon blade is a published method for static balance. The sweep and tamp pad are features of this test fixture. [Du-Bro static propeller balancing guide](https://www.dubro.com/blogs/du-bro-101/du-bro-101-how-to-balance-propellers-on-rc-airplanes)

Use the OpenSCAD file and the geometry JSON to reproduce the fixture layout and locate the blade and backing datums. Use a small scale to verify the trim mass. A side camera with a protractor in its view can measure swing and retention after the arms move clear. A force sensor beneath the backing can confirm the press. The press interval, trim position, actual tool support and both arm clearances are checked from recorded state. The simulated material model represents the removable pickup and permanent adhesion with native constraints. The bond is breakable and activates only after the strip stays on the blade as the pad retracts.

The geometry represents a nylon propeller on a bench balancer. The native solver measures static balance at low speed. Adhesive failure under aerodynamic loads and powered propeller performance are outside the fixture's scope. Replace the strip and restore the original imbalance for each new trial.
