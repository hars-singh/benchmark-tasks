# Take one cup from a spring-fed counter dispenser

Mount a San Jamar EZ-Fit C2410C horizontally with its original trim ring, XC2422SBK small gasket, C2415 spring, and 100458BK pusher. Check the gasket position on an inverted cup using the fit illustration in the San Jamar guide. Load four empty GMZ 9 oz B single-wall paper hot cups small base out. Each cup is 95 mm high with a 77 mm rim and a 52 mm base. Measure the lead-to-next rim pitch of the nested cups before loading. Record the outlet and cup bases from the side with a millimetre ruler parallel to the cup axis. Place a 138 mm square nonslip mat on the adjacent 127 mm high counter riser. Position the grippers outside the dispenser and mat at reset.

- Zero if a second cup fully clears the outer face or a cup falls off the counter. This is checked over the complete trial, including any later recovery.
- Half credit when the leading cup is fully clear and the next cup has advanced at least 2.5 mm and remains retained. Use the initial exposed base position and the measured pitch to locate the second cup's initial base along the outlet axis. Compare it with the newly exposed base in the side video. The new base should finish 77–97 mm outward from the gasket plane.
- Full credit when the only removed cup rests upright and still on its base within the mat, the stack remains retained, and both grippers have cleared the cup by at least 83 mm from its centre. Score after one continuous second. Check the 12-degree orientation tolerance against a plumb reference in the side video. The entire cup outline must be above the mat.

The manufacturer documents a molded tube, replaceable hexagonal gasket and spring follower. Simulation uses six sprung leaves in the gasket plane and a spring on a guided follower. The empty cups have thin tapered polygonal walls, a closed base and a rolled rim. The nominal cup nesting pitch, static friction and spring rates describe the simulator. The real fixture retains its stock cup interfaces and spring. Loads enter the simulated stack only through the follower and cup contacts. Paper compression, wet adhesion and permanent damage are omitted. No cup is kinematically attached to either gripper. The riser supplies a flat destination at service height without supporting the cup during pullout.
